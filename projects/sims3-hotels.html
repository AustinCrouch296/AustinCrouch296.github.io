Hotels & Motels

Work in progress The core hotel systems are built and working in game, but this is an active project rather than a finished release. Later features are still being designed, and everything shown here is subject to change.

Bringing interactive hotels to The Sims 3 for the first time ever!

The Sims 3 never got a proper hotel. The resorts added by Island Paradise treat rooms as props: a guest walks into a tower object and disappears, pricing is fixed by tier, and nothing you furnish makes any difference.

Hotels & Motels replaces that with the real thing. Rooms are recognised from the walls, doors and beds the player built. Guests book a specific room, are given the key to that door, and are checked in by a receptionist who has to be on duty. Rooms are priced on what is inside them, so decorating becomes a pricing decision. Every system was specified in a design document before it was built.

My Role

  • Designed the hotel system end to end and wrote its design document.
  • Designed the check-in and checkout flows around what the player sees and does.
  • Designed the staff systems — shifts, housekeeping priorities, uniforms and staff-only areas.
  • Set the economy rules, pricing rooms on their furnishings.
  • Designed the in-game interfaces, including the uniform picker and lot requirements panel.
  • Built the mod's systems on reverse-engineered knowledge of the original game, verifying each mechanism against the decompiled 1.67 client.
  • Tested each system in game and wrote up the results.

Design Decisions

01 You book at the desk, not in a menu

The sim walks over, queues, and picks a room while being served, so availability is checked when they arrive rather than when you clicked.

02 No receptionist, no check-in

If nobody is on duty then nobody gets a room, the player included. Staffing becomes something to think about, not a detail the game quietly handles.

03 Guests walk out before the room is released

Teleporting them would have been simpler, but a guest vanishing at checkout reads as a bug even when it works perfectly.

04 Housekeepers work slower than they need to

Real cleaning is fast enough that one would service a whole hotel in an hour and idle all day, so they keep tidying. Visibly working beats merely efficient.

05 Guests arrive as people, not room-fillers

A booking might be a couple, a group of friends, a family, or a single parent with children — five group types, each sized to a room and given beds that match who they are. Built on top of EA's own tourist system so they behave like the game's own sims.

06 Groundwork for running your own hotel

Player-owned hotels are not implemented yet, but the systems underneath — per-room pricing, staff rotas, occupancy and takings — are being designed so that ownership can sit on top of them rather than be bolted on later.
