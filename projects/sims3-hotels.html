Bringing interactive hotels to The Sims 3 for the first time ever!

The Sims 3 never got a proper hotel system. The resorts added by Island Paradise treat rooms as props: a guest walks into a tower object and disappears, pricing is fixed by tier, and nothing you furnish makes any difference. That didn't go down well with players, and ever since I've seen plenty of people asking for a real hotel system.

Hotels & Motels does just that. Rooms are recognised from the walls, doors and beds the player built. Guests book a specific room, are given the key to that door, and are checked in by a receptionist who has to be on duty. Rooms are priced on what is inside them, so decorating becomes a pricing decision.

None of this would have been possible without MonoPatching the game. Full credit goes to LazyDuchess and Shapes for making that possible.

My Role

  • Worked out how a hotel should behave in The Sims 3, then designed the systems to match.
  • Designed the check-in and checkout flows around what the player sees and does.
  • Designed the staff systems: shifts, housekeeping priorities, uniforms and staff-only areas.
  • Set the economy rules, pricing rooms on their furnishings.
  • Designed the in-game interfaces, including the uniform picker and lot requirements panel.
  • Built the mod on reverse-engineered knowledge of the original game, checking each mechanism against the decompiled 1.67 client.
  • Played every system in game to see whether it held up, and kept notes on what broke and why.

Design Decisions

01 You book at the desk, not in a menu

The sim walks over, queues, and picks a room while being served. Availability gets checked when they actually arrive, not when you clicked. Back out and the visit just ends, with nothing booked and nothing charged.

02 No receptionist, no check-in

If nobody is on duty then nobody gets a room, the player included. Staffing becomes something to think about, not a detail the game quietly handles.

03 Guests walk out before the room is released

Teleporting them would have been simpler, but a guest vanishing at checkout looks buggy and ruins the immersion.

04 Housekeepers work slower than they need to

Cleaning a room outright is quick, so one housekeeper could get through a whole hotel in an hour and then stand around all day. Instead they carry on tidying. Staff you can see working sell the place better than staff who are simply efficient.

05 Guests arrive as people, not room-fillers

A booking might be a couple, a group of friends, a family, or a single parent with children. There are five group types, each sized to a room and given beds that suit who they are. They sit on top of EA's own tourist system, so they behave like any other sim in the game.

06 Groundwork for running your own hotel

Player-owned hotels are not in yet, but the systems underneath it (per-room pricing, staff rotas, occupancy and takings) are being designed so ownership can sit on top of them rather than get bolted on later.
